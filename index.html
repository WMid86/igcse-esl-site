📚 Research & Resources

Academic foundations behind the Extensive Reading approach.

🌍
The Extensive Reading Foundation
The global non-profit organisation promoting and supporting extensive reading in language education — founded by Day & Bamford using royalties from their landmark 1998 book.
🏠 ERF Home 📊 Evidence ER Works 📖 800+ Paper Bibliography 🔧 Useful Resources

📗 Foundational Books

📘
Extensive Reading in the Second Language Classroom
Day, R. R., & Bamford, J. (1998). Cambridge University Press.
The foundational text. Makes the case for ER as essential practice, covering the cognitive and affective dimensions of reading and offering practical implementation guidance including the famous Ten Principles.
📄 View on ResearchGate →
📙
The Power of Reading: Insights from the Research
Krashen, S. D. (2004, 2nd ed.). Libraries Unlimited.
Krashen's landmark synthesis of free voluntary reading research. Shows that access to books and time to read independently produces stronger gains in reading, writing, grammar, and vocabulary than direct instruction alone.
📄 Free PDF → 📄 ResearchGate →
📕
Teaching Extensive Reading in Another Language
Nation, I. S. P., & Waring, R. (2019). Routledge.
A comprehensive, research-grounded guide covering all aspects of ER: motivation, reading fluency, vocabulary learning, and practical implementation. A major update to the field with current evidence.
🔗 View on Routledge →

🔬 Key Research Articles

🗞️
Top Ten Principles for Teaching Extensive Reading
Day, R. R., & Bamford, J. (2002). Reading in a Foreign Language, 14(2).
The widely-cited article distilling effective ER programs into ten clear principles: reading easy material, reading for enjoyment, self-selection, no comprehension tests, and more. A practical framework for implementation.
📄 Reading in a Foreign Language (UH) →
🗞️
Online Extensive Reading in EAP Courses
Zhou, J., & Day, R. R. (2021). Reading in a Foreign Language, 33(1), 103–125. University of Hawaiʻi.
Longitudinal two-semester study of 57 EAP university students' reactions to ER conducted online. Examines motivation, attitudes, and reading gains — highly relevant to ESL classroom implementation.
📄 Full Article (UH) →
🗞️
Establishing an Extensive Reading Program in a Chinese as a Foreign Language Context
Zhou, J., & Day, R. R. (2023). Reading in a Foreign Language, 35(2), 222–246. University of Hawaiʻi.
Reports how an ER program was built from scratch and examines its effect on students' reading attitudes — directly parallels the DSAS ER Project design.
📄 Full Article (ScholarSpace UH) →
🗞️
Free Voluntary Reading: New Research, Applications, and Controversies
Krashen, S. D. (2004). Paper presented at PAC5, JALT 2004, Tokyo.
Krashen reviews evidence for the comprehensible input hypothesis and free voluntary reading, responding to critics and presenting new studies. Freely available as a PDF from Krashen's own site.
📄 Free PDF (sdkrashen.com) →
🗞️
Self-Determination Theory and Day and Bamford's Top Ten Principles for Teaching Extensive Reading
ERIC / Reading in a Foreign Language (2017).
Links Day & Bamford's ER principles to Self-Determination Theory — explaining why student autonomy, choice, and enjoyment drive reading motivation. Useful for understanding the psychological basis of ER.
📄 Free PDF (ERIC) →
🗞️
Principles Guiding Vocabulary Learning Through Extensive Reading
ERIC (2014). Reading in a Foreign Language.
Examines how incidental vocabulary acquisition occurs during extensive reading, identifying the conditions — repetition, context, and frequency — under which new words are retained without explicit study.
📄 Free PDF (ERIC) →
🗞️
Extending Extensive Reading
Day, R. R. (2015). Reading in a Foreign Language, 27(2).
Day revisits and expands the ER principles in light of new research, addressing implementation challenges including large classes, limited book access, and assessment — the real-world issues teachers face.
📄 Free PDF (ERIC) →

📥 Newest Evidence — Download & Read

The latest peer-reviewed research supporting extensive reading as a powerful, evidence-based practice. Click any title to open the PDF directly.

📄
Learning a Language Through Reading: A Meta-analysis of Extensive Reading Effects
Sangers et al. — Educational Psychology Review, 2025
The most up-to-date large-scale meta-analysis of ER research. Synthesises findings across dozens of studies on the effects of extensive reading on second and foreign language learning.
Open PDF →
📄
Role of Extensive Reading in Vocabulary Development, Reading Comprehension, and Reading Speed
Liu & Saad — Eurasian Journal of Applied Linguistics, 2025
A systematic literature review confirming ER's impact across three key language skills — vocabulary growth, comprehension, and reading fluency. Directly relevant to what we are doing at DSAS.
Open PDF →
📄
Teachers' Insights into the Efficacy of the Reading Circle Project Using Graded Readers
Linde & Daniela — University of Latvia
A teacher-focused study examining what works when using graded readers in class — closely mirrors the experience of our own teachers implementing the DSAS ER pilot.
Open PDF →
📄
The Effect of Graded Readers on Reading Comprehension Skill of EFL Students
Kara — Language Teaching and Educational Research, 2019
Controlled study showing measurable improvements in reading comprehension among EFL learners who read graded readers — evidence graded readers work, not just interesting books.
Open PDF →
📄
The Role of Extensive Reading in Fostering Grammar and Vocabulary Knowledge
Celik — International Journal of Social Sciences & Educational Studies, 2019
Demonstrates that ER builds grammar and vocabulary knowledge incidentally — students absorb language patterns naturally through reading, without explicit grammar instruction.
Open PDF →
📄
The Benefits of Graded Reading
Albay — International Journal of Social Sciences & Educational Studies, 2017
A focused study on why graded readers in particular — rather than authentic texts — are the right tool for developing learners. Covers comprehension, motivation, and language confidence.
Open PDF →
📄
Reading Fluency (Chapter 6)
TESOL — Teaching Reading, Revised Edition, 2020
From TESOL's authoritative teaching guide — explains what reading fluency is, why it matters for ESOL learners, and how sustained reading practice builds it over time.
Open PDF →

🎯 Teacher reminders — ER best practice
⏱️ Protected time
Provide 2 × 15-minute sessions of protected silent reading time each week — non-negotiable.
📖 Model the habit
Read your own book alongside students. When you read, they read. Your behaviour sets the tone.
🚶 Walk & connect
Circulate quietly during reading. Lean in and ask: "What's happening in your book?" or "Are you enjoying it?"
💬 Share your knowledge
If you know their book, mention a character or idea — even one sentence builds a reading relationship.
📊 Check the log
Remind students to log their reading each session. Use this portal to spot students who have gone quiet.
🔄 Support book changes
If a student isn't engaged, encourage them to swap — the right book matters more than finishing the wrong one.
